ORBA · Concierge Care — Strategy Brief

Making the Care Team live: messaging, notifications & concierge service

How to turn the Care Team screen from a static list into a real, two-way relationship — meeting clients on the channels they already use, routing to your team instantly, and layering the concierge touches that make ORBA feel like nowhere else.

Prepared for Dan · ORBA.Health, BangkokConcept & research briefAug 2026

The one-line recommendation

LINE is your primary channel — it's non-negotiable for a Bangkok clinic. Pair it with WhatsApp for expats and medical-tourism clients, keep the ORBA app as the clinical source of truth, and route everything through one unified inbox that pings your care team in Slack and mirrors the conversation back into the app. Start by buying an omnichannel layer to launch in weeks; bring chat natively into the app in phase two. Then wrap it in proactive, data-triggered outreach — that's the part competitors can't copy.

01The concierge principle

Concierge isn't a faster help desk. It's the feeling that one team already knows you and is looking out for you — so you never have to think about how to reach them, wait in a queue, or repeat your history. Every design choice below serves three tests:

  • Meet them where they are. The client uses their own everyday app; ORBA does the work of unifying it behind the scenes.
  • One relationship, remembered. Whoever replies has the full picture — biomarkers, plan, last conversation — and it feels personal, not ticketed.
  • Proactive, not reactive. The most premium moments are when ORBA reaches out first: "we saw your sleep dip this week — here's what we'd tweak."

The messaging plumbing is table stakes. The differentiation is what you do with the always-on channel once it exists.

02The channel decision — start with Thailand reality

This is the most important strategic call, and the data makes it clear. LINE dominates Thailand — roughly 47–56 million users, ~67%+ of the population, making Thailand LINE's second-strongest market in the world after Japan. It cuts across every age and income band. WhatsApp, by contrast, has ~12M Thai users and skews heavily to expats and international communicators. For an affluent, mixed Thai-and-expat longevity clientele, that means a deliberate two-channel core, not a single pick.

03The architecture — "one thread, everywhere"

The trap is running four disconnected inboxes. The concierge pattern is a single omnichannel layer: no matter which app the client uses, it becomes one continuous conversation keyed to their patient record, your team is notified in one place, and the reply lands back on the client's channel and in the ORBA app timeline.

04Build vs buy — concrete options

You don't have to choose forever. The smart path is buy to launch, build to differentiate.

Option A — Off-the-shelf omnichannel inbox (fastest, launch in weeks)

  • Respond.io — Malaysia-based, built for exactly this region. Native LINE + WhatsApp + Messenger + Instagram, AI automation, and a dedicated healthcare vertical. Strong default for a Bangkok clinic.
  • SleekFlow — Hong Kong / SEA focused, strong LINE + WhatsApp + payments; popular with Asian consumer brands.
  • Trengo / Rasayel / Bird (ex-MessageBird) — capable omnichannel inboxes; Bird also offers deeper programmable APIs if you outgrow the inbox.

These give you a shared team inbox, assignment, canned replies, basic automation and Slack alerts out of the box. Best for validating the concierge model quickly before investing in a build.

Option B — Programmable / embed-in-the-app (native concierge feel)

  • Twilio Conversations API + Flex — stitches SMS, WhatsApp, chat and more into one programmable thread; lets chat live natively inside the ORBA app with your own data and UI.
  • Sendbird — chat/messaging SDK purpose-built to embed rich chat (and increasingly AI agents) directly in your app.
  • Sinch / Bird APIs — enterprise messaging infrastructure across channels including LINE.

Best when chat must sit beside the biomarker dashboard, inside your brand, with your data model — the phase-2 destination.

05The Slack side — how the team actually works

Slack is the right internal hub — your team already lives there and it keeps clinicians out of yet another console for routine triage. The pattern that feels concierge from the inside:

  • Rich inbound alert. Every new client message posts to a triage channel with context attached: name, plan tier, last biomarker flags, the message, an urgency tag (AI-classified) and a live SLA countdown.
  • Reply in-thread → client gets it. The assigned team member answers in the Slack thread; it routes back to the client's LINE/WhatsApp and saves to the record. No copy-paste, no app-switching.
  • Ownership & on-call. "Claimed by Dr. Zeth," round-robin or on-call assignment, and escalation if a clinical-urgent tag isn't picked up within minutes.
  • Guardrails. Unanswered-message nudges, a daily digest of open threads, and after-hours routing to whoever's on call — so nothing goes quiet.

06The response model — what makes it feel like concierge

  • A named team, not a helpline. You already have Dr. Zeth, Dan and Anton in the app — lean all the way in. The client messages people, with faces and roles, who greet them by name.
  • Visible, tiered SLAs. Show "typically replies within…" in-app, and hold different promises by urgency (clinical-urgent in minutes; general within hours). Meeting a stated promise is what reads as premium.
  • Async-first, with depth on tap. Most contact is quick text; but a "book a 15-min video with your coach" button in the thread turns any conversation into a consult when it matters.
  • Human-in-the-loop AI. AI acknowledges instantly, triages, and drafts a reply in the team's voice — a human owns and sends anything clinical. Speed of a bot, judgment of a doctor.

07Concierge beyond messaging — the real differentiators

Two-way chat is the enabler. These are the moments that make members tell their friends about ORBA.

◆ Proactive — reach out first
  • "We noticed" data triggers: biomarker drift, a poor-sleep streak, a missed check-in → the team reaches out before the client asks.
  • Pre-visit prep & a same-day post-visit recap with next steps.
  • Supplement refill / re-order nudges tied to the Stack supply counter.
  • Seasonal & travel health ("you're flying next week — here's your protocol").
◆ Personal — one relationship
  • A dedicated care manager who owns the relationship end-to-end.
  • Context that carries: the thread remembers everything, so no one re-asks.
  • Birthday, milestone & "one year with ORBA" touches.
  • Family / household accounts; Thai & English fluently, by preference.
◆ Frictionless — remove every step
  • One-tap booking & priority slots straight from the chat thread.
  • In-app video consults; results delivered with interpretation, not just numbers.
  • Home phlebotomy & lab logistics arranged in-thread.
  • In-app payment via LINE Pay / PromptPay — no invoices to chase.
◆ Premium — the white-glove layer
  • Medical-tourism concierge: airport pickup, hotel & itinerary coordination for visiting clients.
  • 24/7 line for members; second-opinion & global specialist referral.
  • Wearable-data monitoring with proactive alerts ("your resting HR is up 3 days running").
  • VIP scheduling, private lounge, and a real human who answers, always.

08AI as the concierge amplifier

Used well, AI is what lets a small team feel omnipresent — without ever replacing the human judgment clients are paying for.

  • Instant triage & routing — classify urgency and topic, route to the right person, start the SLA clock.
  • Draft replies in your team's voice — the clinician edits & sends; nothing clinical goes out unread.
  • After-hours coverage — answer FAQs immediately with an honest "a human on your team will follow up in the morning."
  • Context on tap — whoever picks up gets an instant summary of the client's history and "what changed since we last spoke."

Ties directly to your existing Ask ORBA screen — that becomes the client-facing AI, while the same engine quietly assists the team behind the scenes.

09Trust & compliance — Thailand PDPA

Health data is sensitive personal data under Thailand's PDPA, which raises the bar — and handled well, becomes a trust asset rather than a burden. The PDPC is also consulting on a healthcare-specific guideline, so expect the standard to tighten.

  • Explicit, per-channel consent to message on LINE / WhatsApp / etc., captured at onboarding and revocable.
  • Keep detailed clinical results in the app, not in third-party chats. Best practice: message "you have a new result — open ORBA to view," with a secure deep-link, rather than sending lab values over LINE.
  • Data processing agreements with every vendor, a named DPO, full audit trail, retention limits and attention to data residency.
Positioning. Make privacy visible: "Your results stay in ORBA. We'll only ever ping you to open the app." For a longevity clientele handing you their most intimate data, that discipline is part of the concierge promise.

11How it shows up in the ORBA app

Concretely, the Care Team screen evolves from a roster into a living surface:

  • Presence & a real thread — "Dr. Zeth · online," typing indicators, read receipts, and a channel badge showing it's synced to LINE / WhatsApp.
  • Everything in reach — attach a result, book a visit, or start a video call from inside the conversation.
  • Proactive cards from the team — the same surface where ORBA reaches out first, so the relationship feels alive even when the client hasn't messaged.
  • A visible promise — "typically replies within 2 hours · urgent clinical within 15 min," so the concierge standard is stated, not just hoped for.
